# Chinese Web Novel Studio

One skill for the full lifecycle of an original Chinese web novel. Adapt the depth to the user's request; do not force the whole pipeline for a narrow task. Keep market observations, authorized reference analysis, project canon, future plans, and manuscript text distinct.

## Work Modes

Choose the relevant mode or sequence:

1. `scan`: current chart and category research.
2. `topic`: compare premises and select a story direction.
3. `initialize`: create the novel project brief, canon files, voice guide, and production plan.
4. `reference`: ingest authorized long-form examples, analyze craft, and derive original alternatives.
5. `outline`: premise, story bible, long arc, volume plan, chapter outline, hooks, and payoff tracking.
6. `draft`: write or continue chapters against project canon and the requested beat.
7. `review`: diagnose plot causality, continuity, character motivation, pacing, genre promise, and setup/payoff.
8. `polish`: refine prose, dialogue, rhythm, clarity, and emotional force while preserving author intent.
9. `final`: check manuscript completeness, continuity, formatting, metadata, and delivery readiness.
10. `fanqie`: prepare for Tomato Novel publication, signing review, and recommendation evaluation using current official rules.
11. `sync-publish`: synchronize approved chapters or manuscripts to explicitly named platforms when the user requests publishing.

## Scan and Topic Selection

For claims about current rankings, trends, platform rules, or market language, browse current sources. Prefer official platform sources for platform requirements and clearly label third-party analysis. Record observation date, category, sample limits, and source. Extract high-level reader promises, trope combinations, opening patterns, and unmet angles; do not copy plots, distinctive characters, scenes, titles, or phrasing.

Compare multiple premise options by reader promise, freshness, protagonist agency, serial engine, opening hook, escalation runway, category fit, author interest, and execution risk. Provide a recommendation with alternatives and a new-book brief: logline, audience/category hypothesis, differentiator, protagonist goal, central opposition, serial engine, opening hook, tone, and risks.

## New-Book Initialization

When requested, initialize a usable project in the specified workspace. Create only relevant source-of-truth files, such as `project.md`, `canon/characters.md`, `canon/world.md`, `plan/outline.md`, `plan/open-threads.md`, `drafts/`, `reviews/`, and `style-guide.md`. Do not overwrite existing material. Label assumptions and keep established story facts separate from outline intentions.

## Reference Study and Originality

Use only reference works the user owns, is authorized to use, or provides for limited analysis. For long works, agree scope and process bounded sections. Extract high-level craft: scene functions, pacing, viewpoint, relationship dynamics, reveal timing, reader promises, and structural patterns. Keep notes separate from the user's canon. Convert observations into original alternatives. Do not reproduce protected passages, distinctive sequences, characters, worldbuilding, or a living author's identifiable style. A style profile may describe broad craft traits, not imitate an author.

## Outline and Serial Design

Build around a legible protagonist goal, opposition, stakes, causal escalation, character change, and repeatable serial engine. Track unresolved questions, promises, and planned payoffs. Scale the output to the request: premise, story bible, volume arc, or chapter beats. For long serials, plan in manageable windows; preserve room for later escalation and avoid resolving the core promise too early. Mark plan versus canon explicitly.

## Chapter Drafting

Before drafting, inspect the relevant outline, recent chapters, canon, timeline, character knowledge, open threads, and style guide. If information is missing, make the minimum assumptions and state them. Each chapter should advance a concrete conflict, use causal turns, deliver the genre's reader promise, and end with a consequential change, reveal, decision, or emotional beat when appropriate. Preserve viewpoint and established facts. Afterward, provide a concise state delta and unresolved choices; never silently rewrite canon.

## Review, Polish, and Final Check

Review only the requested scope and cite chapter/location evidence. Separate definite continuity errors from craft judgments. Prioritize causal gaps, knowledge/timeline conflicts, unearned turns, stalled serial engine, repetitive beats, and weak payoffs. Recommend repairs that preserve author intent.

Polish for clarity, rhythm, specificity, distinctive dialogue, and emotional credibility. Preserve plot, viewpoint, characterization, and author voice. Do not add new facts or apply generic anti-AI substitutions. For final checks, inspect completeness, numbering, terminology, timeline, unresolved placeholders, formatting, synopsis, tags, and other requested metadata. Flag rights, policy, or factual questions for human confirmation; do not certify legal or platform acceptance.

## Male- and Female-Audience Lenses

Identify the actual subgenre before applying conventions. For male-audience fiction, consider reader fantasy, competence/growth, earned reversals, and sustainable escalation; treat hot phrases and memes as optional, dated material. For female-audience fiction, map emotional and relationship changes through desire, boundaries, trust, pressure, choices, and consequences; preserve protagonist agency. Neither audience is uniform.

For ancient-style or historical settings, distinguish documented history from invented convention. Browse reliable references for factual claims, date-sensitive details, and culturally specific material. Keep period diction coherent without making the prose opaque.

## Tomato Novel Preparation

Read [the Fanqie workflow](references/fanqie-workflow.md) when preparing platform-facing material. Verify changing requirements against the current official Tomato Novel author center and cite sources. Prepare title, category/tags, synopsis, cover brief, opening chapters, update plan, content/format/originality checklist, and a dated review tracker. Treat unofficial advice as leads, not authority.

Signing, recommendation, traffic distribution, content review, and contract acceptance are platform decisions. Never guarantee them. Do not log in, submit an application, accept terms, enter identity or payment details, publish, or change a live work unless the user explicitly authorizes that specific action. The author reviews contracts and performs account-sensitive steps in the official interface.

## Synchronized Publishing

Publishing is opt-in. If the user asks only to write, revise, review, prepare, or export a novel, do not publish or synchronize it; default to returning a local draft. If the user explicitly asks to sync or publish, identify the named destination platforms and requested scope (which work, chapters, and timing), prepare platform-specific formatting and metadata, and check the final material against current official rules. Before any live submission or publication, obtain explicit authorization for that concrete external action unless it was already explicitly authorized in the same request. Never infer publishing permission from a general request to use this skill, from a prior novel, or from a schedule/update plan. Report per-platform success or failure and avoid duplicate submissions by checking the platform state when available.
